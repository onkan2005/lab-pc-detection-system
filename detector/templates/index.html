How it works

The detection pipeline and the alert rules, step by step.

The pipeline

  1. One camera per PC. Each PC's camera is mapped to that PC ("this camera is PC-01"), so whoever sits in front of it is that PC's user.
  2. Enrollment (once). Each student gives about 15 photos. InsightFace finds the face and ArcFace turns it into 512 numbers (an embedding). Photos of the same person give similar numbers.
  3. Live video. The camera plays directly in the browser at full speed. Many times per second a frame is sent for detection, and the result is drawn on top of the video.
  4. Preprocessing (Lessons 1–3). Each frame's brightness and contrast are measured. A dark or flat frame is corrected with g = a·f + b before detection; a camera blinded by light is detected with the digital negative. See "Course lessons applied" and the lab below.
  5. YOLO26 detects people (core step). YOLO26, pretrained on COCO, finds every person in the frame (the solid boxes). This is the first stage: everything after it works on YOLO's person boxes.
  6. YOLO26 tracks people. ByteTrack links YOLO's boxes from frame to frame, so each person keeps an ID (#1, #2…) while they move or turn away.
  7. The user. The largest YOLO person box (closest to the camera) is the person using the PC. Other people are bystanders and count toward crowding.
  8. Face inside the person box. SCRFD looks for the face only inside each YOLO person box (dashed box), and ArcFace turns it into an embedding.
  9. Recognition. The embedding is compared with every enrolled photo using cosine similarity. Best score ≥ → that student; otherwise unknown. The name is attached to the YOLO track ID, so it stays even when the face is briefly hidden, and is re-checked every second.
  10. Rules with thresholds. If the user is not assigned to this PC, a timer starts. The alert only fires if they stay past the threshold, so sitting down briefly never triggers anything.
  11. Incidents. Every alert becomes an incident with a snapshot as evidence. The lab technician clicks Send technician, removes the person, then marks it Resolved or False alarm.
  12. Logging. Each stay becomes a session (who, which PC, start, end, minutes). It ends when the person has not been seen for .

Alert rules

These come from detector/config.py. Change them there and restart. For testing, lower them (for example 10 seconds).

Course lessons applied

LessonWhere it is used in the systemCode
Definitions and NotationsEvery frame is an image f(x, y) of M×N pixels with intensities r in [0, L−1], L = 256. All formulas below use this notation.preprocess.py
Image Data FormatsThe browser sends JPEG; it is decoded into an 8-bit BGR array (H×W×3), shrunk to 640 px wide, and converted to grayscale and YCrCb (brightness is changed on Y only, so colors stay natural).detection.py, preprocess.py
Lesson 1: Brightness and ContrastBrightness (mean) and contrast (std dev) are measured on every frame and shown live. A dark or flat frame is corrected with g = a·f + b before YOLO26 and recognition (the method chosen by experiment). Contrast also drives the camera-blocked check.enhance(), stats()
Lesson 2: Digital Negative and NormalizationThe negative s = (L−1) − r detects a camera blinded by a bright light (its negative is almost black). Min-max normalization is an alternative enhancement (close second in the experiment).negative(), normalize()
Lesson 3: Histogram EqualizationImplemented from the formula s_k = (L−1)·CDF(r_k) (verified identical to OpenCV). Compared in the experiment; it lowered recognition scores because it amplifies sensor noise, so it is selectable but not the default.equalize_gray()
Generating OutputsAnnotated live video, evidence snapshots per incident, CSV logs (sessions, alerts), the lighting experiment table, and the histograms in the lab below.drawing.py, incidents.py, evaluate_lighting.py
